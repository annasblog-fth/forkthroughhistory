Arroz con Pato · Serves 4

Las Fiestas Patrias, Jul 2026 Peru Chiclayo

Arroz con Pato

The cilantro-green, beer-braised duck rice of northern Peru, fragrant, festive, and made for celebration

Serves 4  ·  Prep 30 min + overnight marinate  ·  Cook 1 hr 30 min

Flavor Profile

I love the herby flavors given by the cilantro and also the green visuals it provides. The rice has got a nice kick of spice and spices. What makes this dish stand apart from other dishes with various poultry served with rice, is the subtle sweetness the loche squash adds, also, giving a nice texture to the rice as well as pairing up nicely with the crisp scent from the green herbs. Every grain is flavorful after cooking in the marinade from the duck, and the duck, after slowly braising, is nice and tender, falling apart almost. And finally, the Salsa Criolla cuts through the hearty meal, crisp and fresh.

Origin & History

Plates for a Nation's Birthday

The aderezo vegetables cooking down

On the 28th of July, Peruvian streets bustle with parades and music. In the Plaza de Armas, red and white fabrics flutter and pulse in the cool wind. As the event draws out the crowd, you might find various food carts helping hungry tourists and Peruvians to anticucho, papas rellenas, and chicharrón, washed down with some Ponche de los Libertadores, the drink to toast the country's independence. In restaurants and on family tables you might encounter flavors of Ceviche, Ají de Gallina, and Anticuchos, and the fragrant, festive, cilantro-y Arroz con Pato.

For many, Las Fiestas Patrias del Perú, or Peru's Independence Day, marks an important moment in Peruvian identity and history. Las Fiestas Patrias, as you might have noticed, is plural. Plural as in the two consecutive days, July 28 and 29, in which Peru gained its independence from Spanish conquistadors: a day for the liberation, and a day honoring Peru's Armed Forces and National Police.

The earliest known written reference to the dish dates back to 1860. By around 1821, near Peru's independence, the dish began appearing in accounts of French-style dinners that adopted its seasoning. At that time, cooking still relied on the batán (grinding stone) to blend the ingredients that would eventually define the dish. Here are some interpretations I have found: traditional accompaniments include onion, tomato, cilantro, green peas, and sometimes corn, while many northern versions add spinach and cilantro folded into the rice, which gives it its characteristic green color. Beer is a common addition to the broth for extra depth of flavor, and some cooks blend it with a splash of pisco, while home versions from the highlands are sometimes made without beer at all.

The technique to cook Arroz con Pato blesses it with a deep harmonious flavor in every bite. According to one Chiclayana chef, the base is built from a broth made with the duck's bones, which is then used to cook the rice, while the duck itself is seasoned and fried in that same concentrated stock, infusing the rice with rich, savory duck flavor rather than just cooking the two together.

Salsa criolla: onion rinsed in cold water, then lime juice and cilantro

Versions & Interpretations

Arroz con Pato is one very traditional and signature recipe representing culinary cultures of northern Peru, originating in the local farms of Chiclayo, north of Lima. The amalgamation of indigenous Moche cultures, Spanish colonial ingredients, and local herbs created a vibrant green rice and tender duck dish. Although this is a popular northern dish found at many celebratory events, like many historical and popular recipes, over the years it has developed into many interpretations and variations, with many families having their own recipes substituting, adding, subtracting and merging. It, in turn, continues to inspire and evolve Peruvian cuisine with its comforting presence. For example its younger sibling, Arroz con Pollo, rice with chicken.

Ingredients

What You Need

Serves 4. Measurements are guidelines, eyeball and adjust as you go.

For the Duck & Marinade

  • 1 whole duck (small), cut into 4 pieces
  • 1/2 bell pepper, diced
  • 70 g loche squash
  • 1 bunch cilantro
  • 8 tbsp yellow chili paste (ají amarillo paste)
  • 2 tbsp mirasol chili paste
  • Salt, to taste
  • Pepper, to taste
  • 1 tsp cumin
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tbsp garlic paste
  • 1 cup dark beer
  • 1/2 cup chicha de jora

For the Cilantro Dressing (Aderezo)

  • 1 1/2 onions, roughly chopped
  • 1 yellow chili pepper (ají amarillo)
  • 1/2 bell pepper
  • A small handful loche squash
  • Salt, to taste
  • Pepper, to taste
  • 1 small bunch parsley
  • 1 small bunch cilantro
  • 1/2 tbsp garlic paste
  • 1 cup duck or chicken stock

For the Rice

  • 1 kg rice, rinsed clean
  • Blended cilantro dressing (from above)
  • 1 cup of green peas
  • Salt, to taste
  • Duck broth (from above)

For the Salsa Criolla (Side Salad)

  • Red onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 chili pepper, thinly sliced
  • Cilantro, chopped
  • Salt, to taste
  • Lime juice, to taste
Method

Steps

The Night Before

  1. Marinate: First, dice all your veggies and herbs. In a large bowl, combine the duck with the diced bell pepper, grated loche squash, and chopped cilantro. Add the mirasol chili paste, yellow chili paste, salt, cumin, turmeric, pepper, and garlic paste. Pour in the chicha de jora and dark beer. Mix thoroughly so everything is well coated. Cover and refrigerate overnight.

The Next Day

  1. Chop the vegetables: Roughly chop the onion, yellow chili pepper, bell pepper, and a little more loche squash.
  2. Sauté: Heat oil in a pan over medium heat. Add the chopped vegetables and cook for 8-10 minutes, seasoning with salt, cumin, and pepper.
  3. Add cilantro: Stir in the roughly broken cilantro and garlic paste. Cook for 2 more minutes, then turn off the heat and let cool slightly.
  4. Blend: Transfer the mixture to a blender and blend until smooth. Set aside.

Cooking the Duck

  1. Sear: Heat a splash of oil in a frying pan and sear the duck pieces for 4-5 minutes per side, until nicely browned.
  2. Braise: Transfer the seared duck to a pot. The marinade from before also joins the party. Cover and cook for 45 minutes to 1 hour, until the duck is fully cooked through.
  3. Rest: Remove the duck from the pot and set aside in a separate container, keeping a bit of its cooking sauce with it so it stays juicy.

Cooking the Rice

  1. In a pot, add the blended dressing, then stir in the rinsed rice and peas. Now's a good time to adjust the salt seasoning if necessary (just remember the broth is going to get cooked down later).
  2. Simmer: Cook over medium-high heat for about 1 and a half minutes, stirring constantly so it doesn't stick to the bottom. Once the liquid has dropped to nearly the same level as the rice, lower the heat to minimum, cover, and cook for 12 minutes, until moist and tender.

Tip: be careful not to add too much dressing-stock to the rice, or else when it's cooked down the rice will become a slurry porridge, speaking from personal experience :0

Make the Salsa Criolla (While the Rice Cooks)

  1. Combine the thinly sliced red onion, chopped cilantro, and sliced chili pepper. Personally I like to give the onions a little rinse under cold water to dilute some of the sharp flavors. Season with salt and lime juice. Set aside to serve alongside the dish.

To Serve

  1. Generously plate, spoon the steaming green rice, place a portion of duck alongside, finish with the fresh salsa criolla on top, and enjoy all that hard work!
Notes & Tips

Be careful not to add too much dressing-stock to the rice, or else when it's cooked down the rice will become a slurry porridge, speaking from personal experience :0

Storage

How Long It Keeps

How Long Does It Last?

For leftovers, store in any clean, airtight container. It's best served immediately, so the longer it sits in the fridge the riskier it gets; it's recommended to be eaten within 3 days at most.

To reheat: put a few spoonfuls of water into the rice to make it fluffy again. Do not let the dish sit out at room temp for too long, chill the rice immediately when storing.
